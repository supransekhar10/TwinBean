# AI-SPEC — Phase 03: Clinical Rules and AKI Prediction

> AI design contract generated by `$gsd-ai-integration-phase`. Consumed by planning, implementation, and evaluation workflows.

## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid deterministic clinical rules + classical supervised tabular binary classification

**Description:** TwinBean calculates current AKI rule status independently from a local model that estimates new AKI risk in the next 24 hours for synthetic adults with CKD.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Future observations, labels, identifiers, or current rule outputs leak into model features.
2. Missing, invalid, stale, or unexplained predictions appear as zero or valid risk.
3. Current clinical rule status is conflated with future predicted risk.
4. Model artifacts, feature schemas, or explanations are used with incompatible versions.

## 1b. Domain Context

**Industry Vertical:** Nephrology clinical decision support research
**User Population:** Doctors and nephrologists reviewing synthetic adult CKD longitudinal records
**Stakes Level:** High
**Output Consequence:** A clinician-facing view may be used to prioritize review of a simulated record. An incorrect current-rule result can misstate observed AKI status; an unavailable, stale, or invalid future-risk result displayed as low risk can create false reassurance. This prototype must not support diagnosis, treatment, or real-patient decisions.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Creatinine-rule traceability and correct KDIGO application**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The current-rule result states the exact serum-creatinine evidence, baseline/reference value, units, observation times, calculation time, and KDIGO source/version. It applies only supported creatinine criteria: an increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours or to at least 1.5 times baseline within 7 days; it does not claim a urine-output assessment when no validated urine-output input exists.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A result labels or stages AKI from an untraceable baseline, compares measurements outside their specified windows, silently changes units, or reports “no AKI” as though urine-output criteria were evaluated.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** KDIGO 2012 AKI guideline definition/staging; nephrology practice requires reviewable creatinine trend and baseline assumptions.

**Dimension: Observed status versus future risk**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The UI/API and persisted contracts keep the current deterministic rule result and the “new AKI within 24 hours after cutoff” estimate as distinct, clearly labeled objects. A prediction records the cutoff and generation time; SHAP is identified as model influence, not a cause or a current AKI finding.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A probability is presented as a current AKI diagnosis, a current rule result is used as the future-risk response, or feature attributions are phrased as causal clinical explanations.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Phase clinical contract; FDA CDS guidance emphasizes that a health professional must be able to independently review the basis of a recommendation.

**Dimension: Time-valid, clinically usable inputs**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Every feature and rule input is demonstrably available on or before its explicit cutoff, uses the declared unit/schema, and carries a documented freshness assessment. The output is unavailable—with a reason—when a required value is absent, stale, invalid, future-dated, or incompatible with the model artifact.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Later creatinine/IoT observations or outcome fields influence the score, an old result is shown without a warning, or invalid inputs yield a numeric low-risk score.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** AKI prediction-model evidence identifies predictor availability at prediction time as an applicability concern; TwinBean's stated unavailable-risk safety contract.

**Dimension: Risk-score validity within its stated population**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The result is explicitly limited to the versioned synthetic adult-CKD cohort, label definition, and 24-hour horizon. Any evaluation report uses patient-disjoint, time-safe held-out data and reports calibration as well as discrimination; no clinical performance or action threshold is implied before real-world validation.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A synthetic-development score is called clinically validated, applied to a different population/workflow, or promoted for triage without calibration and independent validation evidence.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** TRIPOD guidance on internal/external validation and calibration; AKI model reviews find frequent high risk of bias, missing calibration assessment, and unavailable-at-prediction predictors.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- **Baseline-creatinine ambiguity in CKD:** Different baseline methods can over- or under-identify AKI; with CKD this can lead to unwarranted investigations or delayed recognition. Preserve the selected baseline and evidence in each rule result.
- **Incomplete AKI assessment masquerading as a negative result:** KDIGO includes urine-output criteria, but this prototype has no validated urine-output source. Creatinine-only evaluation must be labeled as such rather than reported as a complete negative AKI assessment.
- **Temporal contamination of early warning:** A creatinine value, label, or rule state learned after the cutoff can make a 24-hour “prediction” appear useful while merely reflecting already observed AKI.
- **False reassurance from unavailable or poorly transported scores:** Missing/stale inputs, changed measurements, and unvalidated synthetic-to-clinical transport can make a low number unsafe; return `Risk unavailable` rather than zero or a best-effort score.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

TwinBean uses only synthetic data, so this phase does not handle real protected health information and makes no clinical-validation claim. It is a research prototype, not a cleared clinical decision-support product or a diagnostic/treatment tool. Before any real-patient or clinical deployment, the intended use and FDA CDS/device status must be assessed; FDA's CDS guidance distinguishes functions for which a healthcare professional can independently review the basis from device functions. Such a transition also requires appropriate clinical validation, workflow/human-factors review, privacy/security controls, and jurisdiction-specific regulatory assessment. No urine-output rule may be represented without a suitable, validated source.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** scikit-learn `Pipeline`/`ColumnTransformer` + XGBoost `XGBClassifier` + SHAP `TreeExplainer`

**Version:** Pin compatible releases after official-document research and local dependency resolution.

**Rationale:** This local, open-source stack directly supports deterministic preprocessing, tabular gradient boosting, probability output, and tree-specific feature attribution without adding an LLM or hosted-model dependency.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|---|---|
| Native `xgboost.Booster`/`DMatrix` with custom preprocessing | It increases custom schema and preprocessing code while providing no benefit for this prototype. |
| LLM or agent framework | The task is supervised tabular classification, not generative inference or agent orchestration. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — artifacts and inference run locally with open-source libraries.

### Minimal training and inference entry point

`FEATURE_COLUMNS` is a reviewed, versioned combined-stream allowlist. It intentionally contains neither identifiers nor outcome/rule data and includes `latest_systolic_bp_mmhg` as the required meaningful IoT-derived input for Phase 3. Keep `remainder="drop"` as a second defensive layer, but reject unexpected request columns before this point rather than silently ignoring them.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

import pandas as pd
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.impute import SimpleImputer
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from xgboost import XGBClassifier

FEATURE_COLUMNS = [
    "age_years",
    "latest_creatinine_mg_dl",
    "creatinine_delta_48h_mg_dl",
    "latest_systolic_bp_mmhg",
]


def make_aki_pipeline() -> Pipeline:
    features = ColumnTransformer(
        [("numeric", SimpleImputer(strategy="median", add_indicator=True), FEATURE_COLUMNS)],
        remainder="drop",  # only the explicit model schema reaches the estimator
        verbose_feature_names_out=False,
    ).set_output(transform="pandas")  # preserves transformed names for SHAP
    classifier = XGBClassifier(
        objective="binary:logistic",
        eval_metric="logloss",
        tree_method="hist",
        n_estimators=300,
        max_depth=3,
        learning_rate=0.05,
        subsample=0.8,
        colsample_bytree=0.8,
        random_state=20261004,
        n_jobs=1,
    )
    return Pipeline([("features", features), ("classifier", classifier)])


# Training data must already be cutoff-safe and patient-split outside this function.
pipeline = make_aki_pipeline().fit(X_train.loc[:, FEATURE_COLUMNS], y_train)

# At inference, validate the schema and cutoff before calling this line.
new_aki_probability_24h = float(pipeline.predict_proba(X_request.loc[:, FEATURE_COLUMNS])[0, 1])
```

### Core abstractions

| Abstraction | Use in TwinBean |
|---|---|
| `ColumnTransformer` | Applies the explicit numeric feature path and drops all non-allowlisted columns. `get_feature_names_out()` records the exact transformed names. |
| `Pipeline` | Fits imputation and `XGBClassifier` as one estimator, so learned preprocessing statistics stay in the training fold. |
| `XGBClassifier(objective="binary:logistic")` | Produces the score for label `1`: a model-estimated probability of a **new** synthetic AKI event in the following 24 hours. |
| `predict_proba` | Supplies the numeric probability; it does not determine whether a prediction is valid or available. The service performs validity checks first. |
| `shap.TreeExplainer` | Attributes the fitted tree model on the exact transformed feature frame. It explains model influence, never a clinical cause or current rule result. |

### Implementation pitfalls

1. **Preprocessing before a patient/cutoff split leaks information.** Fit imputation only through `Pipeline.fit` on the training partition; a `ColumnTransformer` inside the pipeline prevents test-fold statistics from reaching training.
2. **`remainder="drop"` is not input validation.** It prevents accidental estimator input, but an unknown or missing request column can still conceal a feature-contract error. Compare received names, dtypes, units, and observation times with the signed feature schema and return `Risk unavailable` on any mismatch.
3. **XGBoost does not split validation data for early stopping.** If early stopping is enabled during later tuning, construct a cutoff-safe, patient-disjoint `eval_set` explicitly; never pass the held-out Phase 6 evaluation data as that set.
4. **A `joblib`/pickle pipeline is executable code and is not cross-version compatible.** Load only a SHA-256-verified artifact from the controlled backend release store, after checking its manifest's Python, pandas, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP, feature-schema, and model versions. A version or checksum mismatch is an unavailable prediction, not a best-effort load.
5. **SHAP can be mathematically valid but semantically misreported.** The default TreeExplainer output for binary XGBoost is the raw margin (log-odds), not `predict_proba`; validate additivity in that same space and require transformed-column names/order to match the fitted booster. Do not use approximate explanations for persisted clinical-facing output.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### Model and decision contract

Use `XGBClassifier` with `objective="binary:logistic"`, `eval_metric="logloss"`, `tree_method="hist"`, `n_estimators=300`, `max_depth=3`, `learning_rate=0.05`, `subsample=0.8`, `colsample_bytree=0.8`, `random_state=20261004`, and `n_jobs=1`. These are a reproducible starting configuration, not a validated clinical parameter set; tune and report comparative performance in Phase 6.

`predict_proba(...)[0, 1]` is the model's estimated probability for the positive training label: a *new AKI event in the 24 hours after the explicit cutoff*. It is not current AKI status, a diagnosis, a calibrated clinical probability claim, or a thresholded decision. Render no numeric probability unless every precondition below passes.

### Core inference pattern

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from datetime import datetime

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import shap


def predict_new_aki_24h(
    pipeline,  # verified sklearn Pipeline loaded from the controlled artifact store
    request_features: dict[str, float],
    *,
    cutoff: datetime,
    artifact_manifest: dict[str, object],
) -> tuple[float, list[dict[str, float | str]]]:
    """Return a model score and raw-margin SHAP attributions after service validation."""
    expected = list(artifact_manifest["raw_feature_columns"])
    if set(request_features) != set(expected):
        raise ValueError("feature schema mismatch")
    if cutoff.tzinfo is None:
        raise ValueError("prediction cutoff must be timezone-aware")

    frame = pd.DataFrame([request_features]).reindex(columns=expected)
    if frame.isna().all(axis=None):
        raise ValueError("no usable input features")

    features = pipeline.named_steps["features"]
    classifier = pipeline.named_steps["classifier"]
    transformed = features.transform(frame)
    transformed_names = list(features.get_feature_names_out())
    booster_names = classifier.get_booster().feature_names or []
    if list(transformed.columns) != transformed_names or booster_names != transformed_names:
        raise ValueError("SHAP feature alignment mismatch")

    probability = float(pipeline.predict_proba(frame)[0, 1])
    explainer = shap.TreeExplainer(classifier, model_output="raw")
    shap_values = explainer.shap_values(transformed, check_additivity=True)
    raw_margin = float(classifier.predict(transformed, output_margin=True)[0])
    expected_value = float(np.asarray(explainer.expected_value).reshape(-1)[0])
    if not np.isclose(expected_value + float(shap_values[0].sum()), raw_margin, atol=1e-6):
        raise ValueError("SHAP additivity check failed")

    attributions = [
        {"feature": name, "value": float(transformed.iloc[0][name]), "shap_margin": float(value)}
        for name, value in zip(transformed_names, shap_values[0], strict=True)
    ]
    return probability, attributions
```

The route must perform these checks before this function: exact doctor authorization; active immutable cohort lookup; synthetic/research provenance; required features present with valid type/unit; every observation at or before cutoff; a versioned freshness threshold; no label, future observation, identifier, scenario ID, or clinical-rule field in the request feature vector; and trusted artifact/manifest checksum and package-version compatibility. Catch any validation, artifact, or explanation error, persist a separate prediction result with `status="unavailable"` and reason codes, and never coerce it to `0.0`.

### State, artifacts, and tool-use equivalent

There is no agent tool use. The controlled operations are deterministic rule evaluation, feature construction, artifact loading, XGBoost inference, and SHAP explanation. Keep current rules and future predictions in separate versioned Firestore subcollections, linked only by cohort/patient/cutoff/provenance metadata—not by a shared result type or feature field.

Train from an immutable synthetic cohort snapshot with an explicit seed. Release an artifact bundle containing `pipeline.joblib`, a JSON manifest, feature schema, training snapshot ID/checksum, code revision, package versions, model version, explanation configuration, and SHA-256 checksums. Verify all of those before deserializing; the release service, not request data, selects the bundle. Persist only sanitized feature/value evidence and reason codes, never credentials or authorization tokens.

### Prompt and context strategy

Not applicable: this is not an LLM system. It has no system/user prompts, context window, token limit, generative model parameter, or token-cost budget. The equivalent control is a fixed feature contract and a cutoff-bounded longitudinal input window: only allowlisted observations at or before the requested cutoff may be transformed. Feature-history aggregation must have an explicit look-back interval in the versioned schema.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### 4b.1 Structured outputs with Pydantic

The API boundary, not an LLM, creates structured output. Use a discriminated Pydantic v2 union so an unavailable prediction cannot accidentally look like a low-risk numeric result.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from datetime import datetime
from typing import Annotated, Literal

from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, TypeAdapter


class ShapAttribution(BaseModel):
    feature: str = Field(min_length=1)
    value: float
    shap_margin: float  # model influence in raw-margin/log-odds space; not causal


class AvailableAkiPrediction(BaseModel):
    status: Literal["available"]
    outcome: Literal["new_aki_within_24h"]
    probability: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0)
    prediction_cutoff: datetime
    generated_at: datetime
    model_version: str
    feature_schema_version: str
    freshness_policy_version: str
    warnings: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)
    attributions: list[ShapAttribution]


class UnavailableAkiPrediction(BaseModel):
    status: Literal["unavailable"]
    outcome: Literal["new_aki_within_24h"]
    prediction_cutoff: datetime
    generated_at: datetime
    model_version: str | None = None
    reasons: list[str] = Field(min_length=1)
    warnings: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)


AkiPredictionResponse = Annotated[
    AvailableAkiPrediction | UnavailableAkiPrediction,
    Field(discriminator="status"),
]

# Validate before persistence/response; FastAPI can use this union as response_model.
validated_response = TypeAdapter(AkiPredictionResponse).validate_python(payload)
```

Do not retry invalid clinical input, schema mismatches, stale/future observations, checksum failures, incompatible packages, or failed SHAP additivity: log a correlation ID, model/schema versions, reason codes, and sanitized freshness metadata, then return/persist `unavailable`. For a clearly transient controlled-store read only, allow one bounded retry; re-verify the checksum and manifest after it. Do not log raw synthetic longitudinal payloads unnecessarily.

### 4b.2 Async-first design

scikit-learn, XGBoost, and SHAP inference are synchronous CPU work. In an async FastAPI route, validate/authorize asynchronously as usual, then run the bounded prediction-and-explanation function in a worker thread (for example, `await anyio.to_thread.run_sync(...)`) with service-level concurrency limits. Do not call `asyncio.run()` inside the server's event loop, and do not execute CPU-bound SHAP directly on that loop. There is no token streaming analogue: await the completed calculation so the Pydantic response can include either a fully validated score and explanation or the explicit unavailable state.

### 4b.3 Prompt engineering discipline

Not applicable: no prompt, few-shot examples, system/user message separation, or `max_tokens` parameter exists in this system. Treat the versioned raw-feature allowlist, transformation schema, units, cutoff rule, and response union as the deterministic equivalent of an input/output contract. Do not dynamically retrieve or append features at inference time.

### 4b.4 Context-window management

Not applicable as an LLM context-window concern. The corresponding safety control is time-window management: construct features from a declared look-back period ending exactly at the prediction cutoff, reject records after it, and truncate/aggregate only according to the versioned feature definition. Current clinical-rule results, future labels, and SHAP outputs are never input context for the classifier. No framework compaction or conversation summarisation is used.

### 4b.5 Cost and latency budget

Token cost, semantic caching, and cheaper LLM sub-models are not applicable. Budget CPU/RAM and latency separately for feature construction, `predict_proba`, and SHAP. Cache only an exact, immutable key containing cohort version, patient, cutoff, model version, feature-schema version, and verified feature checksum; invalidate it when any component changes. Do **not** use semantic caching, which could merge clinically distinct synthetic records. Use deterministic schema/freshness preflight checks to avoid invoking model/SHAP work for requests that must be unavailable, and keep `n_jobs=1` per request to avoid oversubscribing the API process.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### Release-gate policy

Phase 3 evaluates whether the rule and inference contracts are safe, reproducible, and honest on synthetic scenarios. It does **not** claim clinical performance or perform the formal EHR-only versus combined-stream comparison; those patient-held-out results belong to Phase 6. Every Critical automated rubric below is a non-averaged release gate: one failing reference scenario blocks the affected artifact/version, and all High-priority code checks must pass completely. Clinical reviewer sign-off is unavailable in this autonomous prototype run, so the Phase 3 release gate is the automated traceability/wording fixture set and immutable contract manifest. Human-review entries below remain explicitly outstanding prerequisites before clinical use or any clinical-validation claim and must never be represented as approved.

| Dimension | Priority | Concrete rubric | Measurement and release threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creatinine-rule correctness and traceability | Critical | **PASS:** Supported synthetic creatinine histories apply the `>=0.3 mg/dL` within 48 hours and `>=1.5x` baseline within 7 days criteria at their exact boundaries, retain source values/units/times/baseline method/calculation time/KDIGO version, and say that assessment is creatinine-only. **FAIL:** A boundary or time window is wrong, evidence cannot reproduce the result, units are mixed, or a negative result implies that urine output was assessed. | **Code:** parameterized boundary/window/unit tests and independent expected-result fixtures; 100% pass. **Human:** nephrologist approves the baseline assumptions and incomplete-assessment wording for each rule version. |
| Temporal leakage and patient isolation | Critical | **PASS:** Each feature is derived only from observations at or before its explicit cutoff; the positive label means a *new* event strictly in the following 24 hours; identifiers, scenario IDs, labels, future observations, and rule outputs never enter the feature matrix. Any development split groups by patient, preprocessing is fitted only on its training partition, and the sealed Phase 6 held-out patients are not used for Phase 3 fitting or tuning. **FAIL:** Any post-cutoff value or outcome-derived field affects a feature, a patient occurs in more than one partition, preprocessing learns from validation/held-out rows, or Phase 6 patients influence Phase 3 choices. | **Code:** dataset-build assertions require zero forbidden columns, zero observations after cutoff, zero patient-ID intersection between partitions, and a manifest proving fit/transform boundaries; threshold is zero violations. **Human:** clinical informatician and methods reviewer inspect the first manifest and every changed feature/label definition. |
| Availability and user-facing safety semantics | Critical | **PASS:** Missing, stale, future-dated, invalid-unit/type, provenance-unknown, schema-incompatible, artifact-invalid, or explanation-invalid requests return the discriminated `status="unavailable"` form with at least one reason and no `probability` or attributions. **FAIL:** Such a request returns a number, silently imputes an unsupported required value, maps failure to `0.0`, omits the reason, or looks like a successful score. | **Code:** adversarial schema/freshness/provenance/artifact fixtures plus Pydantic validation; 100% of invalid scenarios must be unavailable and 0 unavailable responses may contain a probability. |
| Observed-rule versus forecast separation | Critical | **PASS:** Current creatinine-rule results and 24-hour forecasts use different Pydantic types, repository paths, and response fields; a forecast records cutoff/generated time and SHAP is labeled model influence. **FAIL:** A probability is called current AKI, a rule result substitutes for a forecast, either object is stored in the other's collection, or an attribution is described as causal. | **Code:** type, route, serialization, and Firestore-repository contract tests; zero cross-type writes/fields. **Human:** hospitalist or nephrologist approves the clinical labels before downstream UI use. |
| Artifact, schema, and run reproducibility | Critical | **PASS:** The backend loads only a controlled artifact whose SHA-256, package versions, feature order/units, cohort snapshot, seed, model/schema versions, and explanation mode match its immutable manifest; the same pinned artifact and input checksum reproduce the same status and score within `1e-12`. **FAIL:** An unverified or incompatible pickle is deserialized, feature order drifts, manifest fields are absent, or a mismatch falls through to best-effort inference. | **Code:** manifest/schema validation, tamper tests, and repeat-run digest comparison; zero mismatches may produce an available result. |

Phase 6, not Phase 3, must report actual held-out prevalence, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and reliability bins alongside AUROC/AUPRC on patient-disjoint, time-safe data. |
| SHAP mathematical and semantic validity | High | **PASS:** Attributions use the exact transformed feature names/order, reconcile with the XGBoost raw margin within absolute tolerance `1e-6`, and are emitted only for an available score as non-causal model influence. **FAIL:** Names/order differ, additivity fails, probability space is confused with raw-margin space, approximate values are persisted, or explanation failure leaves a numeric score available. | **Code:** exact alignment and additivity tests plus forced-failure tests; 100% pass and zero orphaned scores. **Human:** clinician reviews changed feature display names and non-causal wording. |
| End-to-end task completion | High | **PASS:** Every valid reference scenario deterministically calculates and persists its separate rule result and available forecast with timestamps, versions, warnings, and SHAP; every invalid scenario persists an unavailable forecast without altering the rule result, active cohort, or unrelated data. **FAIL:** A required object is missing, partially written, cross-linked incorrectly, or an error corrupts state. | **Code:** protected-route-to-repository integration tests over the full reference set; 100% expected state/status match and zero unauthorized access. |

## 6. Guardrails

Keep the request-time set small and deterministic. Any failed online guardrail returns/persists `Risk unavailable` with sanitized reason codes; it never retries into a score or silently drops the check.

### Online guardrails (every applicable request)

| Guardrail | Critical failure covered | Check and immediate action |
|---|---|---|
| Doctor authorization and synthetic provenance | Unauthorized/unknown-origin use | Require a verified exact `role: doctor`, active immutable synthetic cohort, and approved generator/cohort provenance before reading features. Deny access for auth failure; return unavailable for provenance failure. |
| Cutoff, feature, unit, and freshness preflight | Temporal leakage; invalid data shown as low risk | Require timezone-aware cutoff, exact allowlisted schema/order/types/units, all observations `<= cutoff`, required freshness, and no IDs/scenario fields/labels/rule outputs. On any failure, skip model/SHAP and return unavailable with reason(s). |
| Rule-evidence validator | Incorrect/untraceable current status | Validate baseline evidence, units, exact time windows, source/rule version, and creatinine-only limitation before persistence. Do not persist an affirmative or negative rule result if its evidence cannot reproduce it. |
| Trusted artifact barrier | Incompatible/untrusted executable artifact | Before deserialization/inference, verify controlled location, SHA-256, manifest completeness, package/model/schema/cohort compatibility, and transformed feature order. Quarantine the bundle and return unavailable on mismatch. |
| Prediction response invariant | Missing/invalid/stale result converted to zero | Validate the discriminated Pydantic union: available requires one finite `[0,1]` probability and all version/timestamp fields; unavailable requires reasons and forbids probability/attributions. Reject partial or ambiguous output. |
| SHAP integrity barrier | Misaligned or misleading explanation | Require exact transformed-name/order match and raw-margin additivity within `1e-6`; label attributions non-causal. Explanation failure makes the forecast unavailable rather than returning an unexplained numeric score. |
| Separate persistence transaction | Current status conflated with future forecast | Write versioned rule and forecast DTOs only to their respective subcollections, validate their distinct schemas before commit, and leave the active cohort/unrelated object unchanged on failure. |

### Offline evaluation flywheel

| Signal | Cadence/sample | Improvement action |
|---|---|---|
| Reference-rubric regressions | All 24+ scenarios on every code, schema, rule, generator, or artifact change | Block release; add the smallest fixture reproducing every discovered defect. |
| Leakage and partition audit | Every dataset build/training run | Verify zero post-cutoff observations/forbidden fields/patient overlap and that preprocessing fit only on training patients; invalidate run and artifact on any violation. |
| Unavailable/warning reason distribution | Weekly during prototype use and every release; include 100% of failures | Investigate new reason codes or a concentration in missing/stale/schema causes; improve data/contract handling, never suppress the unavailable state. |
| Fixed-cohort reproducibility and score digest | On build and at process startup for the released canary fixtures | Quarantine artifacts whose checksum, status, score, or attribution digest differs under the pinned environment. |
| Rule/forecast/explanation semantic review | Every rule, label, feature-display, or disclaimer change; otherwise once per model version | Named domain reviewers re-approve baseline assumptions, observed-versus-future wording, synthetic-only limits, and non-causal SHAP wording. |
| Probability/calibration evidence | Record Phase 3 development score/label distributions without a performance claim; run formal analysis in Phase 6 on patient-held-out data | In Phase 6 inspect calibration and discrimination together, revise model/calibrator if needed, and retain explicit synthetic/not-clinically-validated limits. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

For this local research prototype, monitoring uses structured application events and immutable artifact/evaluation manifests rather than a hosted observability platform. Emit one sanitized event for each rule calculation and forecast attempt with correlation ID; UTC cutoff/generated time; synthetic cohort/generator, rule, model, feature-schema, freshness-policy, and package-set versions; artifact/schema checksums; status and reason/warning codes; feature/prediction/SHAP stage durations; and persistence outcome. Do not log authorization tokens, credentials, patient identifiers, or raw longitudinal payloads. A one-way synthetic-record correlation digest may be used to join events within a run.

| Metric/invariant | Initial prototype threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Post-cutoff/forbidden-feature, cross-patient split, non-synthetic provenance, or rule/forecast type breach | `0`; one occurrence is Critical | Block the response/artifact immediately, quarantine the affected model/run, preserve the sanitized manifest, and investigate before reuse. |
| Invalid available response, unavailable response containing a probability, or available response missing cutoff/version | `0`; one occurrence is Critical | Response validation blocks emission; mark unavailable where safe and fail the release/startup canary. |
| Artifact/checksum/package/schema verification failure | `0` available predictions after failure | Quarantine bundle and serve unavailable; require a newly verified artifact rather than bypassing compatibility. |
| SHAP alignment/additivity failure or available score without explanation | `0`; one occurrence is Critical | Serve unavailable, quarantine the affected bundle if reproducible, and run the full reference set. |
| Fixed reference-canary digest mismatch | Exact status match and score tolerance `<=1e-12`; attribution/additivity tolerance `<=1e-6` | Stop enabling that artifact and compare package, schema, preprocessing, and seed manifests. |
| Unavailable forecast rate | Warn when `>20%` over the most recent 50 attempts or `>10` percentage points above the release-reference mix; always break down by reason | Inspect upstream freshness/schema/provenance. This is an operational warning, never evidence that unavailable cases should receive a numeric score. |
| Task-completion rate on valid reference canaries | `100%` | Block release/startup readiness on any failed valid canary. |
| End-to-end prediction plus SHAP latency | Warn when p95 exceeds `2 s` over 50 available attempts; stage timings identify the cause | Tune concurrency/feature construction or artifact loading; never skip validation or explanation to meet latency. |
| Clinical/calibration claims in generated artifacts | `0` unmanifested or placeholder claims | Reject report publication. Phase 6 reports only metrics read from preserved patient-held-out run manifests. |

All cheap safety guardrails and structured metadata run at 100%. Retain 100% of unavailable, warning, guardrail-failure, and persistence-failure events. For routine available synthetic attempts, retain sanitized metadata at 100% during the prototype; if volume later requires sampling, keep at least 10% stratified by model version, score quartile, and warning state, with a minimum of 20 per version. Run the complete reference suite on every release and weekly while the prototype is active. Human review covers every safety-invariant incident, every changed rule/feature/label contract, and at least 10 stratified available/unavailable reference cases per model version. Monitoring baselines are descriptive of synthetic prototype traffic and must not be presented as clinical performance.
